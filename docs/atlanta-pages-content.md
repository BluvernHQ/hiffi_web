## `/atlanta/genres/trap`

**Title:** Trap Music Hub

**Description:** Atlanta trap — from early 2000s Zone 6 streets to streaming-era worldwide sound. Browse eras, essential lists, and related artists.

**Intro:** Trap is Atlanta’s most exported sound: hard 808s, hi-hat rolls, and street storytelling that reshaped global hip-hop. Use this hub to jump into eras, best-of lists, producers, and Hiffi mood mixes built for the same energy.

**Keywords:** trap music, atlanta trap, trap hub, southern hip-hop

### What defines Atlanta trap

Classic trap pairs sparse, menacing production with vivid street narrative. Producers like Shawty Redd, Zaytoven, Lex Luger, and Metro Boomin each pushed the template forward.

On Hiffi, trap energy shows up in aggressive mood mixes — especially On Sight and Turn Up — and across Artist Index profiles tagged trap.

**Title:** Crunk Music Hub

**Description:** Atlanta crunk — club chants, call-and-response energy, and the mid-2000s party sound that ruled Southern radios and arenas.

**Intro:** Crunk turned Atlanta clubs into choirs: shout hooks, sirens, and bass designed to move bodies. It sits between bass/booty culture and the trap explosion that followed.

**Keywords:** crunk, atlanta crunk, lil jon, southern hip-hop

### From clubs to charts

Crunk peaked when Atlanta party records became national anthems. The sound prized live-crowd participation over quiet headphone listening.

## `/atlanta/best-of/essential-atlanta-trap-tracks`

**Title:** 25 Essential Atlanta Trap Tracks

**Description:** A curated starter list of Atlanta trap essentials — street anthems, radio records, and underground heaters that define the sound.

**Intro:** Native Atlanta trap essentials only — 25 tracks sequenced roughly chronologically/thematically. Pair with On Sight and Turn Up mood mixes for the same energy on Hiffi.

**Keywords:** atlanta trap tracks, essential trap, atl trap songs

### Ranked entries

1. **Get Low** — *Lil Jon & The East Side Boyz feat. Ying Yang Twins (2002)*: Crunk-to-trap bridge track, one of the biggest national crossovers to come out of Atlanta's club scene.
2. **No Mo Play In G.A.** — *Pastor Troy*: Self-produced Atlanta anthem, hometown-pride record often cited as a trap-adjacent foundation.
3. **What's Yo Name** — *T.I.*: From the artist who named the genre with his 2003 album Trap Muzik.
4. **Icy** — *Gucci Mane feat. Young Jeezy (2005)*: Breakout single from Gucci's debut Trap House; sparked the Gucci/Jeezy "So Icy" dispute.
5. **Whatever You Like** — *T.I.*: Trap sound polished for pop radio.
6. **Hard in da Paint** — *Waka Flocka Flame*: Lex Luger's maximalist drums, arena-level aggression that influenced EDM-trap crossover.
7. **Versace** — *Migos*: Zaytoven-produced, triplet-flow breakthrough that reshaped mainstream rap cadence.
8. **Type of Way** — *Rich Homie Quan (2013)*: Breakout mixtape single, melodic trap delivery style that influenced a generation.
9. **Constantly Hating** — *Migos feat. Trouble (2013)*: Early Migos mixtape cut, part of the group's ground-floor buzz.
10. **Danny Glover** — *Young Thug (2014)*: Breakout single, one of the clearest early markers of Thug's genre-warping vocal style.
11. **Turn On The Lights** — *Future (2012)*: Breakout single from Pluto, cemented Future's melodic-trap sound.
12. **My Bubblegum** — *Rasheeda*: Snap/crunk-era hit from one of Atlanta's foundational female voices.
13. **Flex (Ooh Ooh Ooh)** — *Rich Homie Quan*: Melodic trap hook that became a singalong standard.
14. **Mask Off** — *Future*: Flute sample and haze-heavy delivery, one of Atlanta trap's most recognizable exports.
15. **Fuck Up Some Commas** — *Future (2015)*: DS2-era street-to-chart crossover.
16. **No Heart** — *21 Savage (2015)*: Early breakout mixtape cut, minimalist and cold, set the template for his sound.
17. **X** — *21 Savage feat. Future (Savage Mode, 2016)*: Metro Boomin-produced, the project that made 21 Savage a name outside Atlanta.
18. **Money Longer** — *21 Savage (2016)*: Another Savage Mode-era standout.
19. **Bad and Boujee** — *Migos feat. Lil Uzi Vert*: Culture-shifting hit, Migos' commercial and cultural peak.
20. **I Get the Bag** — *Gucci Mane feat. Migos (2017)*: Metro Boomin-produced radio smash, a mentor-to-student handoff moment.
21. **Slippery** — *Migos feat. Gucci Mane (2017)*: Same-era release, generational chemistry.
22. **Ric Flair Drip** — *Offset feat. Metro Boomin (2018)*: Metro-produced, culture-defining hook and ad-libs.
23. **1 Night** — *Lil Yachty (2016)*: Bubblegum-trap breakout hit, distinct melodic lane within the broader Atlanta trap sound.
24. **Drip Too Hard** — *Lil Baby feat. Gunna (2018)*: Breakout melodic-trap duo hit, one of the decade's biggest streaming successes.
25. **First Day Out Tha Feds** — *Gucci Mane (2016)*: Post-incarceration comeback single, widely cited as a career-redefining moment — closes the list on a full-circle note given Gucci's presence throughout.
